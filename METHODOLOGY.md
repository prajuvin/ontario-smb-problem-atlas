# Methodology

## What counts as evidence

Every row in the atlas needs a named source, a link, and an as-of date. Sources are ranked by how close they are to the original data.

| Confidence | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| high | Read directly from the publisher's own page |
| medium | Read from a secondary page that summarises the publisher's data |
| low | Reported in search results but not opened |

## What is not evidence

Opinion pieces without data, vendor marketing pages, and figures with no date. These may inspire a question but never become a row.

## From problem to build

A problem becomes a build only when three things are true:

1. The problem has a cited figure or a named owner who described it.
2. A small tool (three to five features) could plausibly reduce it.
3. Someone real can test the tool within two weeks.

Problems that fail the second test, such as the overall tax burden, stay in the atlas as context and are labelled as policy issues.

## Update rhythm

The atlas is reviewed every Friday. Each change records what was added, what was corrected, and why.
